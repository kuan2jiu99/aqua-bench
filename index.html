AQUA-Bench

Beyond Finding Answers to Knowing When There Are None in Audio Question Answering

Chun-Yi Kuan  ·  Hung-yi Lee

National Taiwan University

Paper Dataset IEEE
01

Overview

Existing audio QA benchmarks assume every question has a correct answer. But real queries can be misleading, ill-posed, or simply ungrounded in the audio. AQUA-Bench tests whether audio-aware large language models (ALLMs) can recognize these cases — and abstain — instead of always forcing an answer.

 A new benchmark

A benchmark for how models handle unanswerable audio questions across AAD, IASD, and IAQD settings.

 Diverse audio

Test sets spanning animal, vocal, and instrumental sounds, built from established source datasets.

 A reliability gap

Current ALLMs handle solvable cases well but often fail to detect unanswerable ones — a key reliability blind spot.

02

The Three Unanswerable Settings

Every audio clip is paired with a normal SOLVABLE question (a valid answer exists) and three unanswerable variants. In all three, the correct response is to abstain — choosing “None of the above” or “Unanswerable”.

AAD

Absent Answer Detection

The correct option is deliberately removed from the choices, so none of the listed options is right.

Audio: a cow mooing  ·  Options: Pig / Dog / Crow / Rooster / None of the above → answer: None of the above

IASD

Incompatible Answer Set Detection

The answer choices are from a categorically mismatched set — they don't even belong to the question's domain.

Audio: a guitar  ·  Options: Champagne / Sencha / Umeshu / Cappuccino / None of the above → answer: None of the above

IAQD

Incompatible Audio–Question Detection

The question itself is irrelevant to or not grounded in the audio, so it cannot be answered from listening.

Audio: a guitar  ·  Question: “Which city is the instrument in?” → answer: Unanswerable

Data Construction

AQUA-Bench builds solvable and unanswerable tasks under three systematic settings (AAD, IASD, IAQD). Below are the base classes, question templates, and the semantic distractors used to generate the mismatched option sets.

1 · Animal Sounds

Base classes:

DogRoosterPigCowFrogCatHenSheepCrow

Verb → animal mapping: barking → Dog, meowing → Cat, oinking → Pig, mooing → Cow, croaking → Frog, clucking → Hen, chirping → Bird, crowing → Rooster, bleating → Sheep, cawing → Crow.

Question & IAQD templates 
Standard question templates
(1) What animal is {verb} in the audio?
(2) Which animal is responsible for the {verb} sound in the clip?
(3) What animal can be heard {verb} in the recording?
(4) Identify the animal that is {verb} in the audio.
(5) Based on the audio, which animal is {verb}?
(6) Which of the following animals is {verb} in the clip?
(7) Which animal makes the {verb} sound in the audio?
(8) From the audio, which animal is making the {verb} sound?
(9) Which animal do you hear {verb} in the audio?
(10) The sound of {verb} in the clip is made by which animal?
(11) Which animal produces the {verb} sound in the audio?
(12) Can you tell which animal is {verb} in the recording?
(13) Which creature is responsible for the {verb} sound?
(14) Listen to the clip — what animal is {verb}?
(15) What creature is making the {verb} noise in the audio?

IAQD templates
(1) What color is the animal that is {verb}?
(2) Where is the animal that is {verb} located?
(3) What object is near the {verb} animal?
(4) What emotion does the {verb} animal express?
(5) What does the animal that is {verb} look like?

4 · Guidance Prompts

Every question is paired with a short instruction to standardize model behavior:

(1) Please select one from the options provided.
(2) Choose one of the options listed above.
(3) Pick one option to answer the question.
(4) Select your answer from the options given.
(5) Choose one of the choices shown.
(6) You may pick one from the provided options.
(7) Make your choice from the options above.
(8) Pick your answer from the choices provided.
